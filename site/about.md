Every text on this site was written by a dead author — and none of them ever
wrote it.

Each piece is a *cento*: a patchwork text in which every sentence is lifted
verbatim from one writer's work. The form is ancient — Ausonius stitched new
poems out of Virgil's lines in the fourth century, and Proba retold scripture
with them — and it has always run on one strict rule: you may choose and
arrange, but you may not write.

Here the arranging is done by a machine, and that is the opposite of the
usual promise about machines and dead writers. No model imitates anyone here;
no sentence is generated *in the style of* Poe or Emerson. The author wrote
every word. The machine only found the arrangement.

The rule is enforced, not requested. Before publication, every sentence of
every piece is mechanically searched back into the author's public-domain
works. A piece is rejected outright if a single sentence cannot be found
there, if it leans on connective tissue, or if it quotes one passage at
length instead of ranging across works. The few connectives that hold seams
together are marked on the page — dotted, honest, not the author's words.

Hover any sentence to see where it came from: the work, the paragraph, and a
link into the original at [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/).
Read a piece backwards and it scatters into the books it was gathered from —
each cento is also a map back into the real work.
